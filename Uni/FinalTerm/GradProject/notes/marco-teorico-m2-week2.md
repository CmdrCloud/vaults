# Theory Framework Month 2 Week 2

From your requirements to a presentable technical position: selection criteria,
tables for comparisons, citations without copying, and closing Chapter I.

## from requisites to a technical position

1. requisites and restrictions
2. criteria
3. alternatives
4. comparison table
5. justified selection

- requisites and restrictions are what my TFG is requiring and what it limits
    (what I'm going to use: e.g. revolutionpi)

- criteria are technical, with context, and of cost (why i'm using it)

- alternatives are real options, including what is available today
    (what other options do i have)

- each cell in a comparison table must have data and a source

- and your justified selection must have your technical position that chapter 1
    closes with

---

### criteria

**what is ok (comes straight from your TFG):**

- your requirements: what the solution must do
- your specific objectives: what you're going to measure or demonstrate
- your restrictions: time, budget, infrastructure, and agreements with your org

**not ok:**

- theory framework concepts: knowing what something is doesn't
    tell you what to choose

- riding the hype train: "i use this because i know it"

- tool brochure: what the manufacturer says is not what your TFG needs

---

**example:**

- requirement: personnel register sales even though the internet fails

- question: does each alternative allows you to work without a connection and
    sync later?

- criteria: `<<operation without connection>>:` yes or no, with a source

---

#### three types of criteria

- technical: does the alternative do what my requirement needs?
- contextual: does this fit in the org and for my situation?
- cost: can the client reasonable pay for this?

Each type of criteria has an owner: a requirement, an objective, or a restriction
in my TFG. If you cannot find the owner, it is not a criteria

---

### real alternatives

**what alternatives are ok:**

- at least 2 or 3 real alternatives: options that a reasonable person would
    seriously consider

- what the org currently uses is always an alternative
  - you must be able to compare the two in order to show what value your
    solution brings

- what you already know along with others that have the same criteria

---

### comparison tables

**example:**

```md
CRITERIA: operation without connection (technical), for requirement number 1

A: yes, with differential synchronisation (author, year, p.X)

B: no, requires a permanent connection (author, year, etc.)

Actual/Current Solution: local excel spreadsheet (author, year, p.X)

- A is the only solution because it is the only one that brings in synchronisation
```

---

### anatomy for justification

1. criteria: and the restriction or requirement that it comes from
2. table results: what the table shows, what alternative works, and with data
3. decision: selection in a phrase - what to adopt and why for your TFG
4. risks: what you're accepting when choosing an option

## citing without copying: paraphrasing and citing

**paraphrasing:**

```md
your words and your structure, with the cited source:

The number of the page or the paragraph is not obligatory. It is included when
it helps to find a passage in a long or complex paper
```

**from the author with less than 40 words:**

```md
goes between quotes around a sentence:

The complete citation goes along with the same sentence, with the page or the
location of the passage.
```

**within a block with 40 words or more:**

```md
the source goes between parenthesis after the final period:

Or the author and year before the block and only the page number later.
```
